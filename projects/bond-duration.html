Objective

I started this project to build a practical understanding of interest-rate risk: how much bond prices move when rates change, and how that sensitivity differs across Treasuries and corporate bonds. Duration is the core measure used to study it.

Work completed so far

My original work (summer 2026)

  • Set up an Excel sheet covering Treasuries and corporate bonds.
  • Calculated duration for each bond.
  • Applied simple +100 and −100 basis-point interest-rate shocks to estimate price changes.

That original workbook is not published here.

Corrected workbook (AI-assisted, October 2026)

A separate, corrected workbook was prepared with AI assistance as a learning exercise. It uses synthetic inputs for eight hypothetical bonds, corrects the duration calculations, and completes the ±100-basis-point scenarios with exact repricing comparisons. It is not my original summer workbook, and its inputs are not historical market observations.

Scope and methodology

The corrected workbook covers eight hypothetical fixed-coupon bonds: five Treasury-style bonds (2-, 5-, 7-, 10- and 30-year) and three hypothetical corporate bonds (two A-rated, one BBB). All inputs are synthetic, priced to a settlement date of 1 October 2026.

  • Pricing: Excel's PRICE function, $100 face value, semiannual coupons, and the US 30/360 day count. The day count is a simplification and not a claim about actual Treasury market conventions.
  • Duration: Macaulay duration from the timing of discounted cash flows, adjusted for settlement between coupon dates. Modified duration = Macaulay duration ÷ (1 + yield ÷ 2).
  • Duration estimate: price change ≈ −modified duration × change in yield × dirty price (clean price plus accrued interest).
  • Exact repricing: each bond is repriced at the shocked yield with PRICE, so the estimate can be compared with the exact result.
  • Limits: the duration estimate ignores convexity. Corporate yield shocks do not model credit spreads or default separately, and no portfolio weights are used.
